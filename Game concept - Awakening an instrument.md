# Awakening an instrument

## Overall direction

Build and awaken an impossible musical instrument: something between a synthesizer, a sculpture and a circuit. This provides a light overarching purpose for the puzzle runs without requiring extensive world-building.

Progress brings the instrument into existence and makes it function. The destination is not a collection of revealed pictures: the parts contribute to a shared audiovisual whole that becomes more complete through play.

The exact theme and visual treatment remain open. A literal model of synthesizer components is not required.

## Runs and persistent progression

Each puzzle type uses the shared [[Run director]]: three lives, personalized escalating challenge, and a target of roughly fifteen presented puzzles per run. Skills are assessed separately to adapt challenge and establish learning progress. Runs end when lives are exhausted, or successfully after three correct ceiling-generated answers once every skill's assigned challenge is at its maximum. Successful completion does not require proficiency estimates of 100%.

Types unlock when relevant earlier abilities reach suitable milestones, not necessarily when an earlier type is fully completed.

## Progress as a developing structure

Represent each puzzle type as an area of the larger instrument. Its independently tracked learning dimensions can appear as branching paths or channels rather than conventional progress bars.

One visual reference is molten material flowing through tracks, forming connections and illuminating a structure. Other possibilities include assembling an image, inscribing a living score or forming resonant surfaces. These are exploratory references, not settled art direction.

Milestone activations can unlock further content while an area is still developing. Full illumination could mark completion of its defined targets; the exact completion model and number of milestones remain undecided.

The reveal should feel like construction or activation, not merely removal of fog from an existing image. Areas can have different visual identities while belonging to one coherent object.

## A soundtrack built through progress

The menu begins silent. As areas activate and develop, they contribute audibly to the menu music. The accumulating soundtrack is a persistent expression of what the player has built, and a reason to keep progressing beyond scores or completionism.

Every area should have an audible contribution, but that does not mean every area adds another permanently playing track. Contributions could include voices, phrases, variations, contrasting sections or interactions between existing parts.

A shared arrangement system selects and combines unlocked material. It can alternate contributions and feature different areas, making the piece richer in structure and variety without making it endlessly denser.

The core mapping is:

- Puzzle type → visible area of the instrument.
- Tracked learning progress → developing channels and activation points.
- Area activations → musical material available to the shared arrangement.

## Partial functionality — unresolved

Areas need to contribute before they are fully complete. How partial progress translates into musical behaviour remains to be designed.

Do not adopt a fixed sequence such as dormant, first activation, developing, connected and complete: those proposed stages were not accepted. No particular intermediate-state scheme is settled.

An implementation possibility is continuous visual progress with discrete musical changes, avoiding a bespoke composition for every progress value. The cadence, reliability, musical character and feedback for those changes remain open.

## Rewards and other possibilities

Unlockable instrument sounds could be voices created or awakened within the structure, then made available on the player's chromatic spiral. Procedural sound design, including envelope variation, is a possibility rather than a specified system.

Optional difficulty spikes during runs, rewarding success with an extra life, are another exploratory idea. Their exact form and relation to the theme are not decided.

## Scale and open questions

The opening catalogue currently has five types: [[Note navigation]], [[Imitation]], [[Tonal contours]], [[Rhythm performance]] and [[Interval identification]].

The full catalogue has not been designed. A rough planning estimate of 12–18 total types was discussed, with approximately twenty areas as a useful stress test for the audiovisual system. These are provisional estimates, not curriculum-derived totals or commitments.

Further design is needed for the overall visual identity, partial musical states, arrangement rules, audible feedback on activation, completion criteria and the relation between adaptive skill estimates and persistent world progress.
