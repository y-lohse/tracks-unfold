# Practical outcomes — end-to-end understanding checks

## Role in the curriculum

Layer 1: the practical capabilities the component skills add up to. Layer 2 is [[Curriculum - Skill map]]. Layer 3—the detailed learning progression, milestones, support and readiness evidence—remains to be designed.

These are ten outcomes, not ten additional skills, game levels, projects or a single final exam. Each is an end-to-end check combining skills; simpler versions can recur early, with richer vocabulary and less support later. The three new integration skills T6, H9 and M6 support outcomes 5, 10 and 8 respectively; they are not three extra outcomes.

All agreed skills remain in course scope. Seventh chords, suspensions, additions, extensions and richer rhythm arrive later in the course, not in a separately deferred version.

## Assessment conventions

- Use unfamiliar examples within the vocabulary taught. Distinguish recognition, construction, hearing and interpretation rather than treating one as proof of all the others.
- Supply a pitch reference where needed; absolute pitch is not a target.
- State the musical context and success requirements. Accept all solutions that satisfy them; do not assess artistic quality or require teaching every exception.
- Provide support appropriate to the learning stage, and distinguish supported from independent success.
- Test relationships the player actually determines, not information supplied by the interface.
- Supporting links below identify the main integration skills; their prerequisite links supply the rest. Later variants depend on the corresponding added vocabulary, not on completing the whole map before beginning.
- External-instrument performance is not required. These checks demonstrate understanding inside the app; actual transfer to instrument use is a product-validation question, not something app completion alone proves.

## 1. Decode an unfamiliar chord and account for its notes

**Check:** Given a chord symbol from the taught vocabulary on an unfamiliar root, construct its notes, identify their roles, and realize a specified bass or inversion. Recognize another voicing of the same chord.

**Demonstrates:** Understanding chord structure rather than recalling a shape.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[C12 - Accounting for every chord note]]
- [[T3 - Chord construction on unfamiliar roots]]
- [[C5 - Voicing and doubling]]

## 2. Turn a progression into actual music—and back into relationships

**Check:** Given a key and Roman-numeral progression, determine the chords and their notes. Conversely, given chord names and an established key, express the progression in Roman numerals. Realize specified chord-change timing.

**Demonstrates:** Understanding progression relationships independently of fixed chord names.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]]
- [[H3 - Minor-key chords and progressions]]
- [[H8 - Diatonic seventh chords]]
- [[R5 - Meter and downbeats]]

## 3. Establish the key of a straightforward passage

**Check:** Listen to a clearly tonal passage, locate its tonic using a pitch reference or available notes, identify major or minor, and corroborate the choice using its notes and harmonic ending.

**Demonstrates:** Using evidence to find musical home, not assuming the first note or chord names the key. A reference avoids testing absolute pitch.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[K3 - Identifying a key]]
- [[H4 - Tonal endings and cadences]]

## 4. Move music to another key without changing its relationships

**Check:** Transpose a short melody and accompanying progression to a specified key, preserving rhythm, scale-degree relationships, chord qualities and chord-change timing.

**Demonstrates:** Understanding a musical pattern independently of its original note names.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[T1 - Melody transposition]]
- [[T2 - Progression transposition]]
- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]]

## 5. Reconstruct a short musical phrase by ear

**Check:** After hearing a short phrase, use a supplied tonic or starting pitch to reconstruct its pitches and rhythm. Allow repeated listening; do not require staff notation.

**Demonstrates:** Connecting heard music to notes and timing rather than only manipulating visible symbols.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[T6 - Reconstructing phrases by ear]]

## 6. Understand and reproduce a rhythm within its meter

**Check:** Given a pulse and short rhythm, locate its events and rests within beats and bars, reproduce or construct it, then preserve it at another tempo. Later examples include ties, syncopation and triple subdivision.

**Demonstrates:** Understanding timing relationships rather than memorizing attacks at one speed.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[R1 - Pulse and rhythm]]
- [[R2 - Tempo and relative timing]]
- [[R3 - Durations and rests]]
- [[R4 - Beat subdivisions]]
- [[R5 - Meter and downbeats]]
- [[R6 - Offbeats ties and syncopation]]
- [[R7 - Triple subdivision and compound meter]]

## 7. Relate a melody to its harmony

**Check:** Given a melody, key and progression, distinguish chord tones from other scale notes, identify simple passing or neighboring notes, and complete a fragment reaching a specified destination.

**Demonstrates:** Understanding how melody fits accompaniment, not just which scale contains its notes.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[M2 - Melody notes against chord and key]]
- [[M3 - Passing and neighboring notes]]
- [[M4 - Reaching melodic destinations]]

## 8. Choose harmony that meets a musical purpose

**Check:** Given a short melody and established key, choose basic diatonic chords satisfying explicit requirements, such as containing specified melody notes and producing a taught tonic arrival. Accept all satisfying solutions.

**Demonstrates:** Choosing which chords support a melody; this differs from arranging the notes of already specified chords.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[M6 - Choosing harmony for a melody]]
- [[H5 - Basic harmonic functions]]
- [[H6 - Dominant seventh resolution]]

## 9. Connect chords deliberately

**Check:** Realize a supplied progression using inversions and voicings that retain specified common tones, limit movement between voices or produce a specified bass line.

**Demonstrates:** Choosing how to arrange already specified chords, rather than selecting the progression itself.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H7 - Connecting chord voices]]
- [[C6 - Slash chords and specified bass]]
- [[C8 - Seventh-chord inversions]]

## 10. Recognize simple progressions by ear in an established key

**Check:** With a key established, identify the sequence or degree pattern of a short heard progression drawn from a taught vocabulary. Start with clear root-position triads; later vary voicing and chord vocabulary.

**Demonstrates:** Connecting harmonic relationships to heard music, not only written chord symbols.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H9 - Recognizing progressions by ear]]
