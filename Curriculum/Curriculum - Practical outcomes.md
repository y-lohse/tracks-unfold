# Practical outcomes — capabilities in musical use

## Role in the curriculum

Layer 1: the practical capabilities the component skills add up to. Layer 2 is [[Curriculum - Skill map]]. Layer 3—the detailed opportunities for practice, variation and support—remains to be designed. Read [Learning principles](../Learning%20principles.md) for the design approach.

These ten outcomes describe what growing musical understanding makes possible, within or beyond the app. They are not ten additional skills, game levels, required projects or end-to-end tests. The app need not ask the player to demonstrate each complete outcome: different games can develop and reinforce its component relationships. Simpler uses can recur early, with richer vocabulary and growing fluency later. The integration skills T6, H9 and M6 support outcomes 5, 10 and 8 respectively; they are not three extra outcomes.

Current scope follows [[Curriculum - Skill map]]: M5, T5 and C12 are deferred, and I4 is skipped as a separate topic. Seventh chords, suspensions, additions, extensions and richer rhythm remain in scope. The retained outcomes below describe the broader capabilities; deferred components are not current completion requirements.

## How to read and use these outcomes

- “Example use” describes a situation someone with the relevant familiarity could handle. It is illustrative, not a required puzzle, explanation or assessment.
- Recognition, construction, hearing and interpretation are complementary capabilities. Different games can develop different aspects; one game need not exercise them all.
- Revisit familiar relationships in varied examples to develop understanding, memory and fluent use, with support appropriate to the situation.
- Supply a pitch reference where needed; absolute pitch is not a target.
- Where a use becomes a puzzle, state its musical context and requirements. Accept all satisfying solutions without judging unstated artistic quality or requiring every exception to be taught.
- Supporting links below identify the main integration skills; their prerequisite links supply the rest. Later variants depend on the corresponding added vocabulary, not on completing the whole map before beginning.
- These capabilities can inform music-making outside the app. Keyboard familiarity is encouraged where useful, but external-instrument performance is neither required nor guaranteed by app completion.

## 1. Decode an unfamiliar chord and account for its notes

**Current scope:** chord construction and arrangement remain active. The exhaustive C12 note-accounting component is deferred; retaining this broader outcome does not require adding that task now.

**Example use:** Given a chord symbol from the taught vocabulary on an unfamiliar root, construct its notes, identify their roles, and realize a specified bass or inversion. Recognize another voicing of the same chord.

**Musical value:** Understanding chord structure rather than recalling a shape.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[C12 - Accounting for every chord note]]
- [[T3 - Chord construction on unfamiliar roots]]
- [[C5 - Voicing and doubling]]

## 2. Turn a progression into actual music—and back into relationships

**Example use:** Given a key and Roman-numeral progression, determine the chords and their notes. Conversely, given chord names and an established key, express the progression in Roman numerals. Realize specified chord-change timing.

**Musical value:** Understanding progression relationships independently of fixed chord names.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]]
- [[H3 - Minor-key chords and progressions]]
- [[H8 - Diatonic seventh chords]]
- [[R5 - Meter and downbeats]]

## 3. Establish the key of a straightforward passage

**Example use:** Listen to a clearly tonal passage, locate its tonic using a pitch reference or available notes, identify major or minor, and corroborate the choice using its notes and harmonic ending.

**Musical value:** Using evidence to find musical home, not assuming the first note or chord names the key. A reference avoids testing absolute pitch.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[K3 - Identifying a key]]
- [[H4 - Tonal endings and cadences]]

## 4. Move music to another key without changing its relationships

**Example use:** Transpose a short melody and accompanying progression to a specified key, preserving rhythm, scale-degree relationships, chord qualities and chord-change timing.

**Musical value:** Understanding a musical pattern independently of its original note names.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[T1 - Melody transposition]]
- [[T2 - Progression transposition]]
- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]]

## 5. Reconstruct a short musical phrase by ear

**Example use:** After hearing a short phrase, use a supplied tonic or starting pitch to reconstruct its pitches and rhythm. Allow repeated listening; do not require staff notation.

**Musical value:** Connecting heard music to notes and timing rather than only manipulating visible symbols.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[T6 - Reconstructing phrases by ear]]

## 6. Understand and reproduce a rhythm within its meter

**Example use:** Given a pulse and short rhythm, locate its events and rests within beats and bars, reproduce or construct it, then preserve it at another tempo. Later examples include ties, syncopation and triple subdivision.

**Musical value:** Understanding timing relationships rather than memorizing attacks at one speed.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[R1 - Pulse and rhythm]]
- [[R2 - Tempo and relative timing]]
- [[R3 - Durations and rests]]
- [[R4 - Beat subdivisions]]
- [[R5 - Meter and downbeats]]
- [[R6 - Offbeats ties and syncopation]]
- [[R7 - Triple subdivision and compound meter]]

## 7. Relate a melody to its harmony

**Example use:** Given a melody, key and progression, distinguish chord tones from other scale notes, identify simple passing or neighboring notes, and complete a fragment reaching a specified destination.

**Musical value:** Understanding how melody fits accompaniment, not just which scale contains its notes.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[M2 - Melody notes against chord and key]]
- [[M3 - Passing and neighboring notes]]
- [[M4 - Reaching melodic destinations]]

## 8. Choose harmony that meets a musical purpose

**Example use:** Given a short melody and established key, choose basic diatonic chords satisfying explicit requirements, such as containing specified melody notes and producing a taught tonic arrival. Accept all satisfying solutions.

**Musical value:** Choosing which chords support a melody; this differs from arranging the notes of already specified chords.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[M6 - Choosing harmony for a melody]]
- [[H5 - Basic harmonic functions]]
- [[H6 - Dominant seventh resolution]]

## 9. Connect chords deliberately

**Example use:** Realize a supplied progression using inversions and voicings that retain specified common tones, limit movement between voices or produce a specified bass line.

**Musical value:** Choosing how to arrange already specified chords, rather than selecting the progression itself.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H7 - Connecting chord voices]]
- [[C6 - Slash chords and specified bass]]
- [[C8 - Seventh-chord inversions]]

## 10. Recognize simple progressions by ear in an established key

**Example use:** With a key established, identify the sequence or degree pattern of a short heard progression drawn from a taught vocabulary. Start with clear root-position triads; later vary voicing and chord vocabulary.

**Musical value:** Connecting harmonic relationships to heard music, not only written chord symbols.

**Supporting skills:**

- [[H9 - Recognizing progressions by ear]]
