# Tonal contours

## Role

Develop tonal orientation through choosing notes within a supplied major scale against a clearly established tonal context. Use the shared lives-and-runs structure. This note records the high-level learning concept and unresolved choices, not the prototype's implementation details.

## Player action

Hear the key-setting context and accompaniment, then choose notes to complete a phrase according to visual tension targets. The supplied playable notes belong to the major scale; note names and scale-degree labels provide introductory exposure across different keys. Players need not know scale construction or chord terminology to begin.

The current direction also includes a distinct requirement to find home in some puzzles. Its exact presentation and relationship to contour targets remain to be decided.

## Learning coverage

- [[K1 - Hearing the tonic]] is the primary opening target: recognize and reproduce home after hearing a clear tonal context.
- [[S2 - Scale degrees]] receives introductory exposure through the supplied scale and degree labels. This does not establish independent scale construction or degree-to-note translation.
- Tension matching against accompaniment also introduces relationships later developed in [[M2 - Melody notes against chord and key]]. This contextual exposure does not require prior knowledge of chord names or construction.

## Why tension alone does not establish K1

The backing establishes the tonal context, but choosing a low-tension note does not necessarily identify the tonic. Over C-major backing in C major, C, E and G all meet the prototype's low target. Choosing E can succeed without recognizing C as home. When backing changes within the key, the low band's chord-tone membership changes while the key's tonic remains the same.

There is no objection to supplying unexplained harmonic context. The issue is whether the player's required action actually identifies home rather than merely hearing context in which home is present.

## Retain the distinction between home and tension

The prototype originally separated four bands: home; other settled notes; intermediate tension; high tension. Home and the other settled notes were merged because tonic identity is not reliably a lower amount of tension than the other tonic-triad tones.

Keep the three-band tension model as the current reference rather than restoring home as an extra-low band. The bands are contextual modelling choices, not universal rankings of musical tension.

Two questions remain distinct:

- Which note sounds like home in the established key?
- Which note fits the requested tension target against the current accompaniment?

Asking for home does not require a complete named-role taxonomy for every other note. Introduce the auditory idea of home and attach the term tonic to that experience.

## Current proposal, pending detailed design

Allow a home destination requirement distinct from contour height, for example a home marker on the final slot. Height would specify tension; the marker would specify tonic identity. Ensure any simultaneous requirements are compatible.

Potential variations include finding home in a simple context, matching a tension contour, and combining the two. These are possibilities, not an agreed fixed teaching sequence. Exact markers, placement and generator rules remain open.

A visible degree-1 label can supply the home answer. Distinguish supported learning with that cue from independent recognition by ear; decide how to assess the latter without assuming labelled success proves it.

## Open design work

Arbitrate how home requirements integrate with contour targets, what support is supplied, and how K1 is demonstrated. Preserve the separation between tonic identity and chord-relative tension. Do not require chord terminology lessons simply to provide a tonal backing.
