# Rhythm performance

## Role

Develop pulse, timing, duration, subdivision and meter through rhythmic performance rather than a solve-then-submit puzzle. Use the shared lives-and-runs structure. This is an intentionally open early concept; the interface, generation rules and progression details are not settled.

## Player action

Play any available note at the indicated time, hold it for the indicated duration, and leave the indicated silences.

The instrument offers a fixed scale as a sound palette. Pitch choice is free and unscored; using the same note throughout is always sufficient. Changing notes is optional expression, not another demand. The scale limits the available pitches but does not guarantee every sequence sounds harmonious.

An audible metronome provides the pulse. Visual guidance communicates note starts, durations and rests, with beat and bar structure made legible. The exact presentation remains open.

## Learning coverage

### R1 — Pulse and rhythm

[[R1 - Pulse and rhythm]] begins with playing on a steady pulse. Later, rests and subdivisions make the performed rhythm clearly distinct from the metronome beat.

Eventually explore reduced visual timing guidance over short stretches while keeping the pulse audible, so success depends on maintaining the beat rather than only reacting to individual visual targets. This is a design direction, not a fixed assistance-removal schedule.

### R2 — Tempo and relative timing

[[R2 - Tempo and relative timing]] develops through varying BPM. Occasionally reuse a recognizable rhythmic pattern at a different tempo to make preservation of relative timing explicit, rather than only generating unrelated patterns at different speeds.

### R3 — Durations and rests

[[R3 - Durations and rests]] requires both note starts and releases to matter. Distinguish holding a note across beats, playing briefly followed by silence, and retriggering on the next beat.

Show held-note durations and make silence an intentional part of the contract. Assessing only attacks would leave much of this skill uncovered.

### R4 — Beat subdivisions

[[R4 - Beat subdivisions]] develops by placing note starts between beats, initially on half-beat and then quarter-beat positions. A visible subdivision grid can support understanding while the metronome maintains the main pulse.

### R5 — Meter and downbeats

[[R5 - Meter and downbeats]] develops through recurring beat groups across several bars. Explore visible bar grouping and a distinct first-beat metronome sound or visual emphasis.

Repeat short patterns across bars. Later include patterns with no played note on the downbeat while the metronome still marks it: the first beat exists even when the player is silent.

Vary meter meaningfully so players hear and anticipate the grouping, not merely follow different visual separators. Initial curriculum coverage is 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4.

## Scope boundaries

Keep non-rhythmic performance demands out of the foundational type. Do not require specific pitches, melodic memory or rapid navigation to particular instrument positions.

Prescribed or familiar melodies are a possible later integration variant or separate type, combining pitch navigation and rhythm. They are not required for this concept. Familiar melodies would introduce authored musical material and cannot be assumed familiar to every player.

Rhythmic challenge can come from rests, holds, subdivisions, tempo and tracking patterns across bars without scoring pitch choice.

## Open design work

- Choose the visual presentation and how players see upcoming events, beat positions and bar structure.
- Define generation rules, answer tolerances, onset/release assessment and feedback.
- Decide how errors affect the shared lives-and-runs loop without making motor precision the only evidence of rhythmic understanding.
- Define supported introductions, combinations of demands and proficiency evidence for R1–R5.
- Test whether the performance loop is enjoyable and musically legible with a single repeated note, not dependent on optional melodic decoration.

No fixed difficulty ladder, unlock threshold or complete implementation brief is specified yet.
