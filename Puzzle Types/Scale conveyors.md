# Scale conveyors

## Role

Learn scale construction by defining a mechanism that moves several parcels together. A scale determines both the next destination of each included pitch and which pitches are left alone. Degree-addressed routing extends the same delivery problem to note ↔ scale-degree fluency.

The concept is promoted for further design and testing, not specified or validated gameplay. The main risk remains that scale construction becomes a setup task before a more interesting routing game. Use the shared [[Run director]] when interaction and assessment are developed.

## Player action

Pitch classes are stations. Construct a scale to connect seven stations into a circular conveyor. Pulsing it moves every parcel on that scale forward one scale degree; parcels outside the collection stay put.

Deliver every parcel to its marked destination using the available pulses. One pulse affects multiple parcels, including ones already in useful positions. The notes are not merely collected along a prescribed route: the player reasons about several objects under shared operations.

Construction support, movement previews and undo are proposals, not settled controls. Exploratory moves and a submitted puzzle need distinct treatment when integrated with the run rules.

## Worked example

| Parcel | Start | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | B | D |
| Yellow | F | A |
| Blue | E | F |

The kits are C major and G major. Construct their connections and pulse each once, in a chosen order. Movement is simultaneous; there is no collision restriction in this example.

The correctly constructed routes are:

- C → D → E → F → G → A → B → C.
- G → A → B → C → D → E → F♯ → G.

These completed routes specify the example, not information that must always be supplied to the player.

C then G succeeds:

| Parcel | Start | After C | After G |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red | B | C | D |
| Yellow | F | G | A |
| Blue | E | F | F |

Blue reaches F and stays because F is outside G major. Reversing the order leaves Red at D, Yellow at G and Blue at F♯. Each operation changes which later operation can affect a parcel.

## Teaching construction

Begin after relevant interval and note-navigation familiarity, without requiring chord knowledge or tonic recognition by ear.

Supply the major-scale recipe 2–2–1–2–2–2–1 and, initially, a letter scaffold. For G major, the letters are G A B C D E F G. Following two semitones from E reaches F♯; G♭ has the same pitch but the wrong letter for this position. The final semitone back to G closes the pattern.

Lay connections directly onto the routing board and show their movement consequences. The F♯ choice explains both where an E parcel goes and why an F parcel remains untouched.

Start with one conveyor supplied and another constructed with guidance. Later remove letter and interval support separately and vary roots. Reuse a completed conveyor within a puzzle instead of demanding the same scale before each pulse.

A supplied complete route or full preview is learning support, not independent scale-construction evidence. Validate the named scale and its spelling as well as delivery: malformed scales must not become arbitrary custom routes that happen to solve the logistics problem.

## Degree-addressed routing

This is the agreed direction for developing [[S2 - Scale degrees]] within Conveyors, not a separate quiz or puzzle type. Retain the delivery objective, shared parcel movements and pulse-planning decisions; change how destinations and stations are addressed.

### Degree → note

On a note-addressed board, express a parcel's destination as **degree 6 of G major**. The player must route it to **E**. Interpreting the address determines where the parcel needs to go; choosing and ordering pulses remains the puzzle.

### Note → degree

Begin with a single-key board whose stations are labelled by degree rather than note name. In **G major**, a delivery order specifies **E**, and the player must route its parcel to **station 6**. The two forms reverse which representation is supplied, without adding a question after each move.

### Support and boundaries

- Introduce these forms in major keys after relevant scale-construction familiarity. A supplied conveyor can isolate degree translation from construction when useful.
- A full note–degree legend can support initial learning, then become an optional reference. When translation is the focus, board and destination use different representations rather than permanently displaying every answer as a note–degree pair.
- State the reference key for every degree address. In boards with multiple conveyors, activating a different conveyor must not silently reinterpret a destination's degree number.
- Distinguish **move six scale steps** from **deliver to degree 6**. One is a movement amount; the other is a position relative to the tonic.
- Vary keys and routing problems across repeated play so translation develops beyond a memorized screen location. Exact presentation and assistance remain to be tested.

## Further scale developments

- Small forward and backward degree movements can extend one-step pulses. Three steps backward from A in G major leads through G and F♯ to E.
- Natural minor introduces its own recipe after major-scale and degree foundations.
- Parallel collections change membership and routing.
- Harmonic minor raises the seventh and includes a three-semitone gap. A single scale step is not necessarily a tone or semitone.

Relative major and natural minor produce the **same one-step circular permutation**. Their differing tonic roles can matter through explicitly key-addressed degree tasks, but changing the kit name alone does not teach the distinction. [[Tonal switchboard]]'s fixed-note role targets are the primary complementary direction for changing context around an unmoved pitch.

## Learning coverage and limits

- [[S1 - Major-scale construction]]: constructing the pattern with appropriate spellings.
- [[S2 - Scale degrees]]: bidirectional note ↔ degree translation through the agreed degree-addressed routing forms, alongside degree navigation. Interface and generation details remain provisional.
- [[S3 - Natural minor]] and [[S5 - Raised seventh and harmonic minor]]: later construction directions using their actual patterns.
- [[S4 - Relative and parallel keys]]: partial opportunities through collection membership and key-addressed degrees. [[Tonal switchboard]] is the main home for shared-collection versus shared-tonic transformations and [[K4 - Pitch roles across tonal centers]].

The base board represents pitch classes. Circular wraparound does not demonstrate exact octave/register construction. Structural scale play does not establish hearing tonic, identifying major/minor context, or identifying a key from cadential evidence.

Scale overprinting is not part of this type. Its visual-pattern shortcut concern also applies here: success must eventually depend on player-determined note relationships, not only memorized spatial paths.

## Generation and open design work

Generate target arrangements from valid pulse sequences, then solve the resulting board to check alternatives and intended difficulty. Vary overlapping collections, parcels, direction, degree-step counts and pulse budgets. Reject cases in which available operations act identically on every occupied station or one parcel reveals the whole solution.

Resolve:

- A legible portrait-phone representation of spelling, connections and multiple parcels.
- Whether constructing the route feels integral to the puzzle rather than a prerequisite chore.
- How much preview teaches the mechanism versus supplying the answer.
- Assistance removal and transfer to construction on unfamiliar roots.
- The note-addressed and degree-addressed board presentations, assistance and generation rules that make bidirectional translation useful without replacing the routing problem.
- Puzzle submission, reversible movement, feedback and puzzle-type skill assessment.

The first test should include the teaching support, not just ask experienced musicians to solve a prebuilt routing board.
