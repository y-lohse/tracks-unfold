# Tonal switchboard

## Role

Learn relative and parallel major/minor relationships by changing the tonal contexts of several musical signals through shared controls. In the original form, each signal must reach an auditioned destination context. A complementary fixed-note form asks the player to give an unchanged pitch a requested scale-degree role. Changing context is the means to solve a coupled transformation puzzle, not an exercise in rebuilding scales.

The concept is promoted for further design. Musical realization, interaction, generation and assessment are not yet a behavioral specification. Use the shared [[Run director]] when defining submission, lives and skill assessment.

## Board and objective

Several broadcast stations each play a short passage with a clearly established tonic and major/minor context. Each station also has a destination audition: a different passage establishing the context that station must reach.

Make every station establish the same tonic and major/minor context as its destination within a stated activation budget. Melody, rhythm and voicing need not match the destination recording. This is contextual matching, not note-for-note reconstruction or waveform matching.

A keyboard and instrumental performance are not required. Players can replay current signals and destinations while planning.

## Two musical operations

- **Keep home — parallel:** change between parallel major and minor. C major ↔ C minor. The tonic stays; the note collection changes.
- **Keep notes — relative:** change between relative major and minor. C major ↔ A minor. The note collection stays; the tonic changes.

Use the plain-language descriptions alongside the musical terms during introduction. Demonstrate both operations rather than expecting terminology to supply understanding. In this opening vocabulary, minor uses the natural-minor collection; a raised seventh would break the shared-collection contract of a relative change.

A switch can connect to several stations. Activating it applies its operation to every connected station's current context. Overlapping connections make operations interact. A correct station may need to change temporarily, and operation order can change the final result.

## Worked example

For explanation, the contexts are named here. Listening-led destinations would be auditioned rather than labelled.

| Station | Starting context | Destination context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | C major | C minor |
| 2 | A minor | F♯ minor |
| 3 | E minor | G major |

Two switches and a budget of two activations:

- **A: keep home**, connected to stations 1 and 2.
- **B: keep notes**, connected to stations 2 and 3.

A followed by B succeeds:

| Step | Station 1 | Station 2 | Station 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | C major | A minor | E minor |
| A | C minor | A major | E minor |
| B | C minor | F♯ minor | G major |

B followed by A gives the same outer results but the wrong middle result: station 2 goes A minor → C major → C minor, rather than reaching F♯ minor.

Keeping the notes and then keeping the home is not equivalent to performing those operations in the opposite order. The shared station gives that musical relationship a strategic consequence.

## Fixed-note role targets

This is the agreed extension for [[K4 - Pitch roles across tonal centers]]. A station establishes its context and sounds a marked note. That note stays at the same exact pitch when switches change the surrounding context.

The station's objective is **make this fixed note occupy the requested scale degree**. This can replace its destination-context requirement rather than adding a role-identification quiz after the puzzle. The player still chooses shared switches and their order within a budget; those choices now determine the note's role.

### Introductory example

Both stations have **E4** as their fixed note:

| Station | Starting context | Starting role of E4 | Required role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | C major | Degree 3 | Degree 5 |
| B | G major | Degree 6 | Degree 1 |

A shared **keep-notes / relative** switch changes C major to **A minor** and G major to **E minor**. E4 becomes degree 5 at station A and degree 1 at station B. Neither note moves; the tonal homes change.

The table explains the example, not a requirement to display all role labels during play. This is an introductory board. Later overlapping controls can make a useful change at one station disrupt another, preserving the existing planning problem.

### Musical and interaction boundaries

- Establish each context clearly and sound the unchanged note within it so the player can compare its relationship to home. Preserve the marked pitch and register; do not retune it to follow the scale transformation.
- Begin with relative-key changes that retain the fixed pitch in the collection. A later parallel change may place it outside the scale; define that state and its validity explicitly before introducing such boards. Do not silently assign it an ordinary degree or move it to a nearby scale tone.
- Supply relevant degree references during teaching, then reduce automatic role labels when the player is meant to predict the relationship. [[Scale conveyors]] develops the complementary note ↔ degree translation foundation.
- A role target does not prescribe one destination key unless the available operations and other requirements make it unique. Accept every reachable configuration satisfying the stated role targets and budget.
- Do not equate a degree with a universal tension value. Its relation to the tonic is the target, not an invariant ranking of how settled it sounds over every accompaniment.
- Retain the original auditioned-destination form for tonal-context listening. Fixed-note targets complement it; they need not replace it or appear in every puzzle.

## Teaching and listening

In supported introductions, label contexts and show the retained tonic or shared collection. Begin with individual operations before adding overlapping connections and order-dependent plans.

In listening-led forms:

- Starting contexts may remain labelled as anchors.
- Destination contexts are auditioned rather than named.
- Players compare the current signal with the destination through replay.
- Feedback can reveal labels and explain the transformations after commitment; references can remain available during supported learning.

A destination retaining C as home but establishing C minor calls for a different relationship than a destination establishing A minor. Hearing the desired relationship informs the transformation plan.

Include already-correct stations and unnecessary switches. Do not make changing every station or using every switch a universal solution rule. Listening and planning are both legitimate parts of play; assess patterns across many varied boards rather than demanding that every isolated move independently prove recognition.

## Musical realization

The audio must establish tonal contexts, not merely present isolated chords bearing key labels.

- A parallel change should make the persistence of the tonic audible while changing the mode. Matched rhythm and instrumentation can isolate that difference.
- A relative change must establish the new tonic through the arrangement. Merely beginning the same scale on another note is insufficient.
- Keeping the collection does not require retaining the exact melody, phrase order or arrangement. The resulting passage must establish its new home clearly.
- Introductory relative comparisons stay within their shared major/natural-minor collection. Do not introduce raised sevenths as an unnoticed realization detail.
- Destination and station passages should differ enough to require contextual matching rather than memorizing a recording, while remaining clear and comparable.
- Avoid permanent shortcuts such as every passage's last chord revealing its tonic and mode. Do not equate tonic with the uniquely lowest-tension pitch.

Whether brief passages can make these transformations clear and satisfying is a central prototyping question, not an established result.

## Learning coverage and progression

- [[S4 - Relative and parallel keys]] is the principal structural target: shared collection versus shared tonic.
- [[K2 - Major and minor tonal contexts]] is the listening target, including relative contexts sharing a collection.
- [[K1 - Hearing the tonic]] is reinforced through identifying and comparing tonal homes.
- [[K4 - Pitch roles across tonal centers]] is the target of the fixed-note extension: predict and compare the degree of an unmoved pitch as shared controls change its context.

Use relevant natural-minor and tonic familiarity without imposing perfect mastery of an entire branch. Introduce structural operations with support before asking for unsupported destination recognition.

Fixed-note forms add relevant [[S2 - Scale degrees]] familiarity to the context-changing foundations. Unlike incidental role changes during playback, their role targets make the relationship part of the player's planning. Named-context forms emphasize structural prediction; clear before/after playback supports comparing the same pitch's heard relationship to home. Supplied transformations still do not demonstrate independent scale construction or comprehensive key identification.

Visual and listening-led forms provide different kinds of evidence. Assessment should accumulate across repeated, varied play and account for the support supplied, in line with the shared director. Occasional lucky plans are not a reason to append an isolated recognition quiz.

## Generation and open design work

- Vary starting contexts, destinations, switch operations, overlapping connections and activation budgets.
- For fixed-note forms, also vary marked pitches and requested degrees. Validate role-target solutions, preserve exact pitches through playback changes, and define handling of out-of-scale intermediate states before using them.
- Solve generated boards for reachable destinations and meaningful choices. The existence of a transformation sequence alone does not establish an interesting puzzle.
- Include boards where ordering matters, temporarily changing a correct station is useful, or a supplied control should remain unused.
- Keep networks small and legible on portrait phones. A few audible, consequential transformations are preferable to bookkeeping-heavy networks.
- Vary roots and musical realizations; do not rely solely on transposing a memorized board or recording.
- Resolve whether switches can be reused within a budget, along with reset, undo, preview and submission behavior. The worked example does not establish a universal use-each-switch-once rule.
- Separate assistance and listening difficulty from network-planning difficulty.
- Prototype the audio transformations first, then test whether listening remains useful as players become familiar with the operations.
