# Note navigation

## Role

Teach the structure of the note system and interval construction while introducing the game's three-octave chromatic spiral. Listening is not the primary activity. Use the shared lives-and-runs structure; exact generation rules, parameter ladders and proficiency thresholds remain to be designed.

## Player action

Show the chromatic spiral without note labels, except for one highlighted, labelled starting note. Specify an upward or downward movement in semitones, whole tones or interval terminology. The player chooses the resulting named note from several answer options, rather than selecting a destination directly on the instrument.

Keep the spiral's positions stable. Include octave identifiers in anchors and answers when register matters, including movement across octave boundaries. The spiral spans three octaves.

Example: starting at B3, move up one semitone; C4 is the destination.

## Learning coverage

- [[P3 - Natural-note sequence]]: repeating note order and octave transitions.
- [[P4 - Semitones tones and accidentals]]: semitone and whole-tone movement, natural-note gaps, sharps and flats.
- [[P5 - Enharmonic equivalents]]: equivalent pitch names and context-sensitive spelling.
- [[I1 - Semitone distances]]: construct ascending and descending distances.
- [[I2 - Interval names and construction]]: construct correctly spelled named intervals.
- Reinforces the structural component of [[P2 - Octaves and note identity]], without establishing auditory octave recognition or equivalence.

Does not assess [[I3 - Hearing intervals]]. Optional sound would not itself demonstrate listening competence.

## Musical relationships to teach explicitly

The natural-note gaps B–C and E–F are one semitone. Avoid teaching that B and E cannot have sharps: B♯ and E♯ are valid spellings.

Interval number counts letter names, including the starting and ending letters. Interval quality specifies the pitch distance within that letter span. A third above C must use E: C–D–E. A minor third above C is therefore E♭. D♯ reaches the same sounding pitch in the tuning used here but is an augmented second above C, not a minor third.

The app must teach this spelling logic, not assume players already know it or penalize an unexplained convention.

## Answer contract

- A semitone-only instruction determines pitch distance, not a unique enharmonic spelling. Accept equivalent valid spellings or avoid presenting them as competing single-answer choices.
- A named-interval instruction determines both letter span and pitch distance; assess the corresponding spelling.
- Include octave identity in the contract where relevant; a correct pitch class in the wrong octave is not the requested destination.

## Support and development

- Begin with semitone and whole-tone instructions to develop note navigation.
- Introduce named intervals alongside distance reminders.
- Use named intervals without reminders when independently assessing interval-name understanding.
- Vary starting notes, direction and octave crossings; do not rely on a single memorized route.
- Distinguish supplied information from demonstrated understanding. Success with a semitone reminder is not independent evidence of recalling a named interval's size.

These are supported variations within one type, not a fixed authored sequence or separate unlockable types.

## Open design work

Define answer-option generation, teaching feedback, difficulty parameters and unlock evidence. Test whether spatial reasoning and evolving demands sustain enjoyable runs; do not assume multiple-choice correctness alone establishes engaging gameplay.
