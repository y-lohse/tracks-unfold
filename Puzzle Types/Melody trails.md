# Melody trails

## Role

Find a melodic route through a supplied harmonic setting. The player uses note, scale and chord relationships to reach a destination rather than constructing a scale or phrase solely to demonstrate its formula.

The foundation is retained for further design, not yet a behavioral specification. Use the shared [[Run director]] when the question forms and assessment are developed.

## Player action

The accompaniment, key and some melodic landmarks are supplied. Fill the missing melody to reach the destination while meeting a small set of visible requirements.

Possible requirements include staying within a pitch range, using steps or limited leaps, making a turn, passing through a marked chord tone, or arriving at a specified time. Start with supplied timing so pitch decisions remain accessible; later forms can let the player determine durations and rests too.

A note path or a few response slots could be the answer surface, with a keyboard for entry or audition. The exact presentation is open.

## Concrete example

In a supplied C-major setting, a four-note path starts at E4 and must finish at G4. Stay in the scale, make exactly one change of direction, and use no leap larger than a third.

Both E4–D4–F4–G4 and E4–F4–A4–G4 satisfy those requirements. The point is finding a route, not guessing a preferred melody. The starting and ending slots have supplied timing in this introductory example.

Later, a marked note might need to belong to the chord sounding at that moment, or the destination might be the tonic on a particular downbeat. These add musical relationships to the route without requiring every note to be a chord tone.

## Difference from Harmony fitting

**Trails supplies the chords and asks the player to choose the melody.**

[[Harmony fitting]] supplies the melody and asks the player to choose the chords beneath it. The fixed and editable layers are reversed.

Keep Trails and Harmony fitting as separate, complementary puzzle types. They may reuse presentation or interaction patterns, but finding a melodic path is not the same task as selecting an accompaniment.

## Relationship to Tonal contours

[[Tonal contours]] provides an earlier intuitive entrance through home and contextual tension targets. Trails uses more explicit movement, membership and destination relationships.

Do not make those formal demands prerequisites for the existing Contours concept. Sharing a visual language is possible without collapsing the two answer contracts.

## Sources of variation

- Key, supplied accompaniment and pitch range.
- Starting note, destination and intermediate landmarks.
- Repetitions, steps, leaps and turns.
- Marked chord-tone requirements versus unmarked notes.
- Simple passing and neighboring constructions in stated harmonic and rhythmic contexts.
- Supplied rhythmic skeleton versus player-determined event timing.

Changing a target chord can change which paths work even when the available scale remains the same. Vary relationships rather than relying only on longer phrases or larger note supplies.

## Answer boundaries

- State exactly which notes must satisfy chord or scale membership; non-chord tones are not automatically mistakes.
- Distinguish scale steps from semitone distances when expressing movement rules.
- Define the destination and timing requirements visibly. Accept every satisfying path.
- When teaching passing or neighboring notes, provide the harmonic and rhythmic context needed to identify that relationship.
- In minor contexts, make the applicable collection or raised-seventh allowance clear.
- Generate at least one complete valid path and check alternatives against the requirements, not an authored continuation.

## Learning coverage and readiness

- [[M1 - Melodic movement and contour]]: recognizing and constructing repetition, steps, leaps and contour. Formal forms build on I1/S2 and earlier [[Imitation]] experience.
- [[M2 - Melody notes against chord and key]]: determining chord membership separately from key membership, after C1/H1.
- [[M3 - Passing and neighboring notes]]: explicit constructions and recognition after M1/M2/R4.
- [[M4 - Reaching melodic destinations]]: meeting combined destination, pitch and rhythm requirements after M1/M2/K1/R5.

These are potential contributions, not automatic full coverage. A scale-filtered palette supplies membership; a locked rhythm supplies timing. Independent recognition or timing evidence must not be inferred from actions that only select pitches.

## Open design work

- Choose a small vocabulary of visible route requirements that produces interesting alternatives without becoming a list of instructions.
- Determine which relationships can be learned through feedback and which need introductory support.
- Keep the editable melody clearly distinguished from the supplied accompaniment, complementing Harmony fitting's reversed responsibility.
- Test the shortest useful paths and how changes in accompaniment affect their choices.
- Settle input, preview, feedback and puzzle-type skills without turning every curriculum ability into an extra quiz question.
