# Harmony fitting

## Role

Find an accompaniment that fits a supplied melody and reaches a stated harmonic destination. Chord and key knowledge serve a multi-solution fitting problem rather than symbol construction for its own sake.

The foundation is retained for further design. This note does not settle the interface, generator or assessment. Use the shared [[Run director]] when those details are developed.

## Player action

A melody, established key and chord slots are supplied. Choose chords beneath the melody so specifically marked notes belong to the chords supporting them and the ending meets the puzzle's requirement.

The important choices interact: a chord that supports a local melody note may not allow the requested ending or other route requirement. Early puzzles can supply a small chord palette; later ones can require more independent selection or realization of the chosen chords.

Placing a chord could let the player hear it with the melody. Exact preview and submission behavior remains open. A keyboard is optional; chord pieces may be the main interaction.

## Concrete example

In C major, three chord slots align with marked melody notes E, D and C. Use diatonic triads, contain each marked note in its supporting chord, and finish with V–I.

Both C–G–C and Am–G–C satisfy the contract. The first chord can differ because both C major and A minor contain E. The ending fixes the relationship of the last two chords.

This simple example exposes the central decision. Later puzzles should make local fit and the destination interact over more than one choice, rather than merely ask the player to select a chord containing one note repeatedly.

## Difference from Melody trails and Voicing sculpture

- **[[Melody trails]]:** chords are fixed; choose the melody.
- **Harmony fitting:** melody is fixed; choose the chords.
- **[[Voicing sculpture]]:** chords are already chosen; arrange their notes to fit placement requirements and, in multi-chord forms, movement requirements.

Keep Trails and Fitting as separate, complementary puzzle types. They may reuse presentation or interaction patterns while preserving which musical layer the player determines.

## Sources of variation

- Melody, key and number of chord slots.
- Which melody notes must be chord tones; unmarked notes may be non-chord tones.
- Available chord vocabulary and how much chord-note information is supplied.
- Taught ending or functional-route requirements.
- Major first, then minor and appropriate seventh chords.
- Fixed versus player-chosen chord-change positions in later forms.

Construction can become a means to making a chosen accompaniment work. It need not be a compulsory symbol-entry step after every choice, especially when that would interrupt the central fitting loop.

## Answer boundaries

- Make required melody-note membership and the ending explicit. Do not imply every melody note must belong to its chord.
- Accept every progression satisfying the stated requirements; do not score an unstated preferred accompaniment or artistic quality.
- If functionality is constrained, define it within the taught tonal context rather than treating all chord uses as universal.
- Chord identity and voicing are separate. Do not assess unspecified inversion or voice-leading choices.
- Generate compatible melody/harmony material and independently validate player alternatives.
- Membership highlights can support learning even when they supply the local fit. Preserve choices about the complete accompaniment and ending rather than highlighting a single winning sequence. Distinguish supported success from independent membership reasoning only when making that assessment claim.

## Learning coverage and readiness

- [[M6 - Choosing harmony for a melody]] is the primary target: select chords satisfying note-membership and ending requirements. Begins after relevant M2/H2/H4 familiarity.
- [[H5 - Basic harmonic functions]] can be exercised when the player chooses a functional route rather than following supplied function labels.
- Applies [[M2 - Melody notes against chord and key]] and chord vocabulary used by the question.
- Minor forms add [[H3 - Minor-key chords and progressions]]; richer chords require their specific foundations.

H5/H6 completion is not an entry requirement for basic M6. Choosing a V7–I ending alone does not establish [[H6 - Dominant seventh resolution]]: tracking the tendency tones requires an additional task, more naturally developed in Voicing sculpture's multi-chord forms.

Make marked-note membership noticeable through the placed chords, relevant note displays and playback. Choosing and comparing fitting accompaniments is the learning activity; players need not explain why each note fits. If an assessment later claims independent membership reasoning, define proportionate evidence separately rather than adding a compulsory explanation task.

## Open design work

- Find a clear presentation of local fit and phrase destination without reducing the puzzle to automatic matching highlights.
- Test whether players enjoy hearing alternative valid accompaniments and whether the requirements create meaningful choices.
- Choose the size and support level of the opening chord palette.
- Keep the editable harmony clearly distinguished from the supplied melody, complementing Melody trails' reversed responsibility.
- Define evidence, generator rules and difficulty progression after the central loop is clearer.
