# Interval identification

## Role

Connect heard pitch relationships to interval names and semitone distances. Complements imitation: players identify a heard relationship rather than reproduce it. Use the shared lives-and-runs structure. This is a high-level concept, not a full generator or implementation brief.

## Player action

Hear two notes and identify the distance between them. Present notes successively or simultaneously; request an interval name or a semitone count. The exact answer interface remains open.

Track presentation and answer vocabulary as separate dimensions within one type. Successive examples can ascend or descend. Simultaneous examples have no played direction: measure the interval between the lower and higher pitch.

## Learning coverage

- [[I3 - Hearing intervals]]: distinguish successive and simultaneous interval sounds and connect them to their construction and names.
- Reinforces [[I1 - Semitone distances]] and [[I2 - Interval names and construction]] by linking their structural knowledge to sound.

[[Imitation]] develops auditory reproduction; [[Note navigation]] develops structural navigation and naming. This type supplies the explicit sound-to-distance/name connection and simultaneous listening component missing from those concepts.

## Answer contract

Sound alone cannot establish enharmonic spelling in the tuning used here. An augmented second and a minor third, for example, can sound identical.

- Semitone-count answers do not depend on enharmonic spelling.
- For named answers, establish a conventional taught answer vocabulary and do not offer acoustically identical spellings as competing single-answer options.
- If assessing a spelling distinction, supply note spelling explicitly. Such tasks belong more naturally in note navigation than in audio-only identification.
- Interval names accompanied by semitone reminders provide support; distinguish that success from independently connecting sound to the interval name.

## Open design work

Choose the response interface, listening support, example-generation rules, feedback and proficiency thresholds. Vary musical examples so recognition generalizes beyond memorized recordings. Test whether the identification loop sustains engaging runs. No fixed difficulty ladder or unlock route is specified yet.
