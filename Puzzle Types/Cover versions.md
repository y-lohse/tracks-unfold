# Cover versions

## Role

Practise progression and melody transposition by completing several recordings from one shared, limited inventory. Chord rounds use whole-chord cards; melodic rounds use note clips and, in later forms, explicit durations and rests. Choose destination keys that let every recording be completed. A locally correct transposition can consume material needed elsewhere, so preserving musical relationships serves an inventory-allocation puzzle.

Keep chord and melody rounds separate initially. They share the allocation mechanic without requiring a combined melody-and-accompaniment arranging task. The melodic extension is agreed; no separate melody-transposition puzzle type is required for this direction.

The concept is promoted for further design. Interaction, generation, assistance and assessment remain provisional. Use the shared [[Run director]] when defining submission, lives and progression.

## Chord-progression form

The player receives:

- Two or more short source progressions, each with its original key and chord names.
- A stated set of permitted destination keys for each progression.
- One shared hand of chord cards, including any duplicate copies.
- A destination slot for each chord event in each recording.

Choose a destination key for each recording and fill its slots with chord cards, preserving the source's Roman-numeral pattern, chord qualities and order. Each card fills one slot and cannot also be used in another recording. Repeated chord events require separate copies of the corresponding card.

Complete every recording using the available hand. The opening form uses an exact inventory so a successful solution uses every card. The player can plan and rearrange placements before submission; detailed editing and commitment gestures remain open.

Cards represent whole chords, not notes to decompose or perform. A keyboard and external-instrument dexterity are not required. Auditioning source and candidate versions should let the player hear the preserved relationships in different keys; playback details remain to be designed.

### Chord example

Two source progressions:

| Recording | Original key and chords | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | D major: D–G–A | I–IV–V |
| B | E major: E–C♯m–A | I–vi–IV |

Either recording may be covered in **C major or G major**.

The six-card hand is:

**C, C, D, F, G, Am**

Recording A has two locally possible versions:

- In C major: **C–F–G**.
- In G major: **G–C–D**.

Choosing A in C consumes C, F and G, leaving C, D and Am. Recording B would need C–Am–F in C major or G–Em–C in G major. Neither can be completed from the remaining cards.

Instead:

| Recording | Chosen destination | Completed version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | G major | G–C–D |
| B | C major | C–Am–F |

Both preserve their original relationships, and all six cards are used. There are two physical C cards, one for each recording; either copy can fill either C slot.

The unsuccessful choice for A was musically correct. Its problem was consuming the only F needed by B, not an invalid transposition. Distinguish strategic allocation problems from musical errors in feedback.

## Melodic form

Supply two or more visible source melodies, each with its source key and rhythm, permitted destination keys and a shared inventory of note clips. The source remains available throughout play; reconstructing it by ear is not required.

Choose destination keys and assemble transposed versions that preserve each melody's degree pattern, interval direction, register relationships and rhythm. The player selects the actual destination notes; choosing a key must not automatically fill the response. Scarce clips create the same interacting allocation choices as the chord form.

### Staged rhythm support

1. **Opening form:** timing remains attached to the destination slots, and the player selects pitches. This isolates melodic pitch transposition while the result retains its rhythm automatically.
2. **Later form:** each note clip has a fixed exact pitch and duration, with separate rest clips. Assemble them into an empty response strip, preserving the source's attacks, holds and silences. Clips are used whole, once each; repeated events require separate copies.

The later form asks the player to preserve a supplied rhythm, not invent a new one. A one-beat E4 is not interchangeable with a half-beat E4 merely because their pitches match. Show source notes and durations through a legible strip or piano-roll representation; fluent staff-pitch reading and real-time performance are not required.

### Melodic example

Two short source melodies in 3/4:

| Melody | Source key and notes | Durations in beats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | F major: F4–G4–A4–rest | ½, ½, 1, 1 |
| B | G major: G4–B4–D5–rest | ½, 1, ½, 1 |

The degree patterns are **1–2–3** and **1–3–5**. Either melody may be covered in **C major or D major**, with destination tonics **C4 or D4**. These register anchors make the exact destination pitches unambiguous.

The shared inventory is:

| Clip | Duration |
| --- | --- |
| C4 | ½ beat |
| D4 | ½ beat |
| E4 | ½ beat |
| E4 | 1 beat |
| F♯4 | 1 beat |
| G4 | ½ beat |
| Two rest clips | 1 beat each |

A has two locally valid versions:

- In C: **C4(½)–D4(½)–E4(1)–rest(1)**.
- In D: **D4(½)–E4(½)–F♯4(1)–rest(1)**.

Choosing A in C leaves E4(½), F♯4(1), G4(½) and one rest. B can no longer be completed in either allowed key. Its C-major version needs C4(½) and E4(1); its D-major version needs D4(½) and A4(½).

The complete allocation is:

| Melody | Destination | Cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | D major | D4(½)–E4(½)–F♯4(1)–rest(1) |
| B | C major | C4(½)–E4(1)–G4(½)–rest(1) |

Every clip is used, both rhythms still fill three beats, and both melodies preserve their exact pitch intervals and rhythmic relationships. The choice of key remains strategic even with a degree reference available.

## Learning through supported play

Begin with familiar major-key chord–numeral relationships, without waiting for complete minor-key coverage. Early source progressions can show their numeral patterns alongside their chord names. A key reference can support destination realization while the shared inventory still leaves meaningful choices. Learning comes from the allocation decisions, repeated transpositions and audible comparisons, not a preliminary lesson. Corrections or occasional tips can name relationships the player has already encountered.

A later form can omit the supplied source numerals so the player determines the pattern from the source key and chords, then realizes it in a chosen destination key. Vary reference support separately from increasing the allocation problem; retaining a useful reference is compatible with worthwhile transposition practice. Do not make the player rebuild each chord from notes as a prerequisite to placing it.

In chord rounds, attach timing to the source slots in the opening form. The new chords retain the original change positions and durations automatically, allowing attention to stay on harmonic transposition. This preserves timing in the result without claiming independent rhythm construction or performance.

Melodic rounds begin with relevant scale-degree and melodic-movement familiarity. Supply degree references and source timing as needed, then fade those references separately from introducing pitch-and-duration inventory. Start with major-to-major transposition; add corresponding minor material after its foundations are accessible. Neither full minor coverage nor chord-progression mastery is an entry gate for simple major melodies.

Playback can compare source and cover using controlled timing and voicing so the shared harmonic pattern is audible. It supports learning; identifying an unlabelled progression by ear remains the distinct focus of [[Progression Wordle]].

## Sources of variation

- Source keys, source progressions or melodies, and permitted destination keys.
- Shared chords or note clips, duplicate quantities and repeated events.
- For melodic forms, exact pitch registers, rhythmic patterns, rests and clips sharing a pitch but differing in duration.
- Several locally feasible versions competing for the same cards.
- Alternative complete allocations, all accepted when they satisfy the requirements.
- Short repeated patterns versus progressions with different endings.
- Minor-key progressions and seventh chords as the corresponding relationships become accessible through play.

Start with two short recordings and few key choices. Additional songs and options can create bookkeeping rather than better play; complexity should come from interacting allocations, not merely a larger inventory.

## Answer boundaries

- Keep each recording in one selected destination key. Do not switch keys between slots merely to use convenient cards.
- Preserve chord degrees, qualities and order. A recording may not be reharmonized simply because another sequence sounds acceptable.
- Make source and destination mode explicit. Introductory major-to-major forms and later corresponding minor-to-minor forms are transpositions, not relative/parallel mode changes.
- For minor material, state the numeral and raised-seventh conventions so qualities such as v versus V remain unambiguous across keys.
- Card identity includes its chord quality and any taught symbol distinctions. Do not treat chords with the same root but different qualities as interchangeable.
- For chord rounds, exact inversion, doubling and register are not player requirements unless separately specified in a future form; the opening puzzle is not a voicing task.
- For melodic rounds, preserve the ordered degree pattern and exact interval direction and size within the stated source/destination modes. Specify destination register anchors or explicit allowed whole-phrase octave placements; do not allow individual octave substitutions that alter the melodic shape.
- In the pitch-and-duration form, preserve event timing, note lengths and rests as well as pitch relationships. Do not alter the melody or rhythm just to consume a convenient clip.
- Accept every complete key assignment and card allocation satisfying the contract, not only the generator's chosen solution.

## Learning coverage and readiness

- [[T2 - Progression transposition]] is the principal target of chord rounds: realize the same harmonic relationships and chord qualities in different keys.
- [[T1 - Melody transposition]] is the principal target of melodic rounds: preserve the degree pattern and rhythm while selecting actual pitches in a new key. Opening forms supply timing; later clip-based forms require the player to preserve it.
- [[S2 - Scale degrees]], [[M1 - Melodic movement and contour]] and [[R3 - Durations and rests]] support the melodic form. Minor forms add relevant [[S3 - Natural minor]] familiarity.
- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]] supplies and receives practice in the chord-name ↔ numeral relationships used by those transpositions.
- [[H3 - Minor-key chords and progressions]]: later minor covers preserve natural-minor chord relationships and the raised-seventh v/V distinction. For example, v–i in A minor, Em–Am, becomes Bm–Em in E minor; V–i, E–Am, becomes B–Em. Minor coverage is not an entry gate for major-only play.
- [[H8 - Diatonic seventh chords]]: later major-key covers preserve seventh qualities and degrees, including viiø7. For example, Bm7♭5 on degree seven in C major becomes C♯m7♭5 on degree seven in D major.

These are vocabulary extensions of the whole-chord allocation puzzle, not new games. [[Chordfall]] handles the corresponding key-specific derivation and construction.

[[Numeral dominoes]] introduces and exercises chord–numeral relationships within a stated key. Cover versions makes preserving an entire progression across source and destination keys the central demand, with shared inventory giving the choice of key a strategic consequence.

Melody transposition is addressed by the explicit melodic form, not inferred from chord roots moving in chord rounds. Neither form independently demonstrates chord-note construction or voice leading. Slot-supplied timing remains support; reproducing the source rhythm with clips is not independent rhythm composition.

[[Imitation]] already provides complementary exact pitch-relationship transposition practice. A later tonal and rhythm-aware extension there could provide ear-led practice, but is not required by this promotion and does not change Imitation's current specification.

Evaluate patterns across repeated puzzles with varied keys, progressions, inventory and support. Legitimate deduction about scarce cards can contribute to a solve. A strategic dead end is not automatically evidence that the player failed to understand the transposition; exact assessment weighting remains open under the shared director.

## Generation and open design work

- Generate a complete feasible set of covers and an exact shared inventory, then solve across the permitted key assignments and piece multiplicities. For melodic clip forms, match exact pitch and duration together, account for rests, and validate register anchors and full rhythmic preservation.
- Check for consequential choices: some locally valid versions should compete for scarce cards, rather than each recording having an immediately independent answer.
- Vary allocation structure as well as keys; merely transposing the same memorized inventory problem is insufficient variation.
- Keep hands and source/destination relationships legible on portrait phones.
- Resolve placement, key selection, audition, rearrangement and submission gestures without turning every placement into a separate theory question.
- Define assistance that supports learning source interpretation and destination realization through play while preserving the allocation decision; use corrections and occasional contextual tips rather than a lesson sequence.
- Refine the transition from pitch-only melodic slots to pitch-and-duration clips. Keep source rhythms visible without automatically filling the response or requiring a separate rhythm-composition mechanic.
- Provide feedback distinguishing wrong chord relationships from valid versions that leave the remaining inventory unusable.
- Test whether completing a small set of covers feels like a satisfying allocation puzzle rather than an inventory worksheet.

Only Cover versions is promoted here. Linked mazes and Transposition rally remain separate, unpromoted proposals.
