# Chord draft

## Role

Learn to recognize chord qualities by auditioning candidates and recruiting the best team for a particular performance. A shared musical brief defines what makes a candidate valuable; an adversary competes for the same candidates. Whole chords are the pieces, not notes to construct or perform on an instrument.

The concept is promoted for further design. The shared brief, alternating picks, one reserve and final performance are settled foundations. Scoring values, opponent behavior, interaction details and generation remain provisional. Use the shared [[Run director]] when defining submission, lives and assessment; competitive scoring does not establish a separate run system.

## Fixed round structure

1. **Reveal one shared brief.** Both players always pursue the same public requirements. The brief changes between rounds, not between players within a round.
2. **Audition a shared market.** Each candidate is a playable chord. Its root is visible, but its quality must be recognized from sound. Players can replay auditions; a listening timer is not part of the concept.
3. **Alternate picks.** Each player recruits one available candidate per turn. A recruited card is no longer available to the opponent. Competition for a finite cast is the source of external pressure.
4. **Choose the lineup.** Each player has exactly one reserve beyond the number of performance positions. The opening proposal is four recruits for three positions.
5. **Give a final performance.** Players select and order their performers, audition their arrangement, then lock it in. Both performances play and receive scores under the shared brief. The higher score wins the match; tie handling remains to be defined.

The reserve supports backup plans, recovery from uncertain auditions and strategic denial. It is a real selection decision, not an additional performer. Candidate illustrations, colors and other non-audio properties must not encode quality. Showing roots avoids turning the task into absolute-pitch identification.

## What makes the best team

A brief states a small number of objective musical requirements before any drafting begins: initially one principal requirement and at most one or two bonuses. Explain unfamiliar requirements with controlled audio examples.

Possible briefs include:

- A requested sequence of qualities.
- Several minor chords on different roots.
- Major/minor counterparts sharing roots.
- A collection spanning several taught qualities.
- A balanced collection containing specified counts of requested qualities.

These are explicit collection and arrangement goals, not universal judgments of musical quality. Variety is not inherently better, and a sequence of chord qualities is not automatically a functional progression. Do not use a hidden aesthetic or tension score.

The brief makes the value of a candidate contextual. A sound can fill a missing role, enable a bonus, preserve an alternative lineup or deny an opponent a needed recruit. Correct recognition informs those choices without requiring a separate quality quiz after every pick.

## Worked brief and drafting choice

**Tonight: Contrast**

Perform three chords in this order: **major → minor → major**.

- **2 points** for each position containing the requested quality.
- **+1 point**, awarded once, for at least one adjacent major/minor pair sharing a root.
- **+1 point** if the first and third positions both contain major chords with different roots.

Maximum: eight points. These values are illustrative, not settled balance constants. Bonuses can stack.

Suppose a player has recruited C minor and F major, recognized through their auditions. Available candidates include sounds that are actually C major, D major and G minor; these qualities are not written on the market cards.

Recruiting C major enables:

**F major → C minor → C major**

This earns six points for the requested qualities, one for the C-minor/C-major pair, and one for the different major roots: **eight points**.

Recruiting D major instead enables F major → C minor → D major: **seven points**, without the same-root bonus. G minor does not fill the missing major position, but could serve as a reserve or deny an opponent a needed candidate. The remaining draft turns and opponent's recruits affect which choice is most valuable.

The example describes a mid-draft decision, not a complete generated market. Accept and score every final lineup according to the same disclosed rules.

## Final performance and feedback

The final performance resolves the draft. Ordering matters when the brief uses positions or adjacency, and the unused reserve makes the final selection meaningful. With a small lineup, arrangement may be straightforward; the principal strategy is the contested draft, not an additional card-battle system.

After both players lock in, demonstrate how each performance satisfied or missed the brief. When a quality was mistaken, play the selected chord and a controlled counterpart of the requested quality, and explain the relevant structural difference. Feedback should connect the sound with its structure, rather than merely display a lost point.

Playback timing is supplied. This is not a rhythm-performance task, nor does assembling the lineup require keyboard dexterity. Multiple bookings, asymmetric briefs, combat powers and card-activation rules are not part of the promoted concept.

## Teaching and progression

Begin with major/minor contrasts and controlled voicings. Named reference sounds and explanations remain available while players learn; their support can fade as recognition develops. The draft should offer meaningful choices before independent recognition is established.

Progress from same-root comparisons to varied roots, then introduce register, inversion and timbre changes gradually. A quality must not become associated with one memorized recording, register or instrument. Avoid changing several unfamiliar acoustic dimensions at once.

Add diminished and augmented triads after introducing their sounds and interval structures. Later briefs can use common seventh-chord qualities with relevant teaching and references. More qualities expand the collection possibilities; they should not arrive merely to increase strategic difficulty.

## Learning coverage and assessment over repeated play

The principal target is [[C3 - Hearing triad qualities]]: recognizing major, minor, diminished and augmented sounds, with teaching and feedback connecting them to structure. Introductory major/minor rounds support the listening comparisons associated with [[C1 - Major and minor triads]]. Later vocabulary can exercise the listening distinctions in [[C7 - Common seventh chords]], without claiming its construction and notation abilities from drafting alone.

Skill evaluation draws on patterns across many varied games, not a verdict from one isolated match. Lucky collections, accidental denial and occasional strategic wins are normal parts of play; the design does not need to eliminate them or append a separate assessment quiz to every choice.

Use accumulated fulfillment of quality requirements, selection and placement decisions, and the support available to inform longitudinal assessment. Match wins and losses also reflect strategic and opponent difficulty. Exact evidence weighting belongs to further design under the shared director, not a new per-round mastery test.

The enduring design requirement is that listening remains useful across repeated play. Systematic visual answers or a permanently exploitable market pattern would undermine that requirement in a way that occasional luck does not. Vary roots, realizations, candidate distributions and briefs rather than guaranteeing an inferable one-of-each-quality cast.

## Generation and open design work

- Generate finite markets with meaningful competition, viable scoring opportunities and alternative lineups. Not every desired combination must remain available after contested picks.
- Keep the brief shared in every round. Vary its musical requirements across rounds within the player's taught vocabulary.
- Test the four-recruit, three-performer opening format and a market legible on portrait phones. Preserve exactly one reserve if lineup size changes.
- Define opponent strength, turn order and first-pick balance. Increased strategic strength is not automatically increased auditory difficulty.
- Resolve candidate replenishment or a fixed market, audition gestures, information visibility after recruitment, and when quality labels are revealed during supported play.
- Define ties and how competitive outcomes and requirement fulfillment map to puzzle success under the shared run rules.
- Test that bonuses produce real choices without overwhelming the principal listening task. Retain explicit, predictable scoring rather than hidden musical judgments.

## Related candidate retained: Semitone relay

A separate promising direction is a small network of chord stations with fixed roots and fifths. A linked move lowers one station's third from major to minor while raising its neighbor's third from minor to major. Reach a requested quality arrangement within a move budget; routing a change can require temporarily altering an already-correct station.

Its likely role is structural understanding: showing why changing a third changes the chord quality. A visual implementation should not be treated as an independent chord-hearing task. This candidate is retained for later exploration, not promoted as a second puzzle type or incorporated into the draft's rules.
