# Puzzle concepts — portfolio review and refinement guide

## Authority and scope

Read [Learning principles](../Learning%20principles.md), the project [README](../README.md) and the [curriculum skill map](../Curriculum/Curriculum%20-%20Skill%20map.md) alongside these concepts.

**Imitation and Note navigation are implemented, accepted games. Their current implementations are authoritative.** Correct a conflicting concept document to describe the game; do not change the game to satisfy older prose or this review. Their explicit assessment, listening, submission and run contracts are not templates that every new concept must copy, nor are they overridden by general guidance about exploration or support.

The other documents have different levels of detail but remain concepts, not validated gameplay or complete implementation briefs. An agreed extension establishes a direction, not an obligation to implement every variation in the first playable version. Curriculum coverage describes opportunities for learning, not a checklist of required assessments or player explanations. The shared run director remains the authority for run integration; this review does not redesign progression, proficiency or unlocks.

This is a high-level design assessment, not a report of playtesting. It covers all fifteen concepts; it does not settle their individual interfaces, balance or generators.

## Overall assessment

The portfolio broadly follows the learning principles. No core mechanic needs replacing on that basis.

- **Practice is usually integral to the objective.** Chord contents permit removals, scales move parcels, transposition allocates scarce material, and harmonic relationships constrain fitting and routing. The musical action generally changes the puzzle rather than buying admission to an unrelated game.
- **There is useful diversity.** Listening, direct reproduction, structural navigation, performance and strategic construction complement one another. Imitation, Navigation and the proposed direct listening/performance games do not need an added strategy layer merely to resemble the board puzzles.
- **Most concepts already separate musical validity from strategic usefulness.** Preserve this in feedback: a valid chord, cover or voicing can be an unhelpful move without being a theory error. Multi-solution puzzles should accept every answer satisfying their disclosed requirements.
- **Varied repetition has promising foundations.** Change musical material and consequential choices, not only board size or transposition. Returning to familiar relationships is useful for memory; every round need not introduce novelty.
- **Keyboard use is appropriately selective.** Keep it recognizable where pitch placement helps. Whole-chord cards, note pieces, lanes and networks are legitimate alternatives, particularly on portrait phones.

The main uncertainty is not curriculum alignment but whether each proposed loop is satisfying, understandable and worth replaying. A worked solution proves much less than an enjoyable game. Some documents describe a strong puzzle mechanism; others still describe a task or representation whose pleasure and choice structure need to be discovered through play.

## Corrections applied at this review

1. **Support is not something every concept must eliminate.** Lighter references can invite recall, but retained references, previews and comparisons can support productive practice. Judge whether support leaves worthwhile musical attention and decisions, not whether it yields a clean assessment.
2. **Observation is not a compulsory explanation task.** Let sound, note relationships and consequences expose what changed. Labels and short tips can help; avoid automatically converting designer-facing facts into introductory lectures or post-solve questions.
3. **Learning value and proficiency evidence remain separate.** In particular, supported harmony fitting, supplied chord tones and tonal-context exposure remain useful without independently proving every related skill. Tonal contours must not conflate a low-tension answer with finding the tonic.
4. **Implemented behavior takes precedence over stale prose.** The Imitation and Note navigation notes now state that boundary and correct discrepancies found in a bounded source/test inspection. This was not an exhaustive implementation audit or a reason to reopen accepted games.

These changes preserve the existing core mechanics and stated musical boundaries. They do not make unrestricted previews, undo or unlimited listening universal requirements; new concepts still need explicit interaction contracts.

## Shared risks to watch in play

- **Strategic overload:** routing, drafting, inventory and constraint solving can crowd out the musical repetitions they are meant to support. Prefer a few consequential decisions over more pieces and rules.
- **Symbol-only play:** structural games need not assess hearing, but audition and relevant comparisons can connect their symbols to music. Conversely, do not force a sound-identification test onto every structural move.
- **Unclear musical judgments:** tension, function and voice movement need explicit contextual requirements. A puzzle restriction is not a universal rule of good music; the generator's preferred solution is not the answer contract.
- **Premature breadth:** seventh chords, extensions, unknown keys, duration inventories and multi-chord forms are valuable directions, but cannot substitute for making the opening loop enjoyable.
- **Phone bookkeeping:** multiple sources, registers, stations or histories can overwhelm a narrow screen before the underlying music becomes difficult. Test the intended portrait layout with actual input and audio.

These are design risks, not proof that any concept fails. Likewise, omitted assessment coverage is not itself a gap to fill with another game or quiz.

## Handoff for future AI refinement

The next pass should take **one concept into one small playable form**, so feedback can come from playing rather than resolving every possibility in prose. Do not treat this portfolio as authorization to build every type or every extension at once.

For the selected concept, leave a short, explicit handoff in its own document:

1. **Preserve:** its current objective, defining musical mechanic, settled answer boundaries and agreed non-goals. Mark genuinely unresolved choices separately.
2. **Choose the first playable scope:** one form, limited vocabulary and a phone-sized board or phrase. State which later extensions are deliberately absent. Do not build a generic engine or complete difficulty ladder to anticipate them.
3. **Specify the complete loop:** what the player sees and hears, their gestures, what an action changes, available support, audition/edit/reset behavior, commitment, success/failure, feedback and how another puzzle begins. Resolve local attempts versus run submission explicitly; do not invent a competing lives system.
4. **Make the music checkable:** define valid answers independently of the generated witness. Include a worked success and a useful contrast—an invalid answer, a legal but strategically unhelpful move, or an alternative valid solution as appropriate. For direct recognition or performance games, a strategic branch is not required.
5. **Make it replayable enough to judge:** provide several meaningfully different examples or a small validated generator. Repeat the core relationship while varying material and, where relevant, decisions. Include the beginner support in the playable version, not as promised future work.
6. **Make one design question testable:** identify the main uncertainty and what play would reveal. Temporary choices should be labelled as prototype decisions, not silently promoted to permanent requirements. Leave fine-grained proficiency mapping provisional where it is not needed to test the loop; preserve existing run behavior.
7. **Deliver play, not just a plan:** give a clear way to launch the playable version, identify what is and is not implemented, and validate answer checking and relevant input/audio behavior. Record whether portrait-phone play was actually checked. Do not claim learning effectiveness from passing software tests.

After playing, the most useful feedback is concrete: **What did I try to achieve? Which decision was interesting or tedious? What musical relationship did I hear or notice? Where did the rules or feedback surprise me? Did I want another round?** These are optional prompts for the designer's feedback, not questions the game must ask its players.

Update the concept from that evidence before adding more vocabulary, strategy or assessment detail. The goal is a succession of small playable decisions, not increasingly exhaustive specifications that postpone play.
