# Numeral dominoes

## Role

Learn chord-to-numeral relationships by connecting whole-chord dominoes into a playable progression. Chords are the pieces; the player does not decompose them into notes or perform them on an instrument.

The core concept is promoted. Its objective, musical bonuses and scoring remain provisional. Use the shared [[Run director]] when defining submission and assessment; points do not establish a new run system by themselves.

## Core interaction

A stated key applies to a finite hand of directed dominoes. Each piece has a chord name on its left and a Roman numeral on its right. Join a piece to one whose left-hand chord realizes the preceding piece's right-hand numeral in that key.

In D major, D → vi can connect to Bm → I because B minor is vi. Case and quality markings matter; degree numbers identify roots, while chord numerals specify whole chords.

Dominoes are directed, not freely reversible. A piece is used at most once. Arrange and rearrange the chain before submission; exact editing and listening gestures remain open. A keyboard is not required.

Each domino represents a transition. In playback, shared endpoints sound once: D → vi joined to Bm → I produces D–Bm–D, not D–Bm–Bm–D. The terminal numeral is realized as the final chord even without another domino. Initial playback can use supplied equal durations and predictable voicing; this does not assess performance timing or voice leading.

## Proposed objective: clear the hand and fulfill musical goals

Retain using every piece in one legal chain as the introductory puzzle objective. Branching connections and shared chord names can create locally legal choices that strand remaining pieces. Do not rely on hand size alone for difficulty.

Returning to tonic need not be the objective of every puzzle. A fixed hand and starting chord may already force the final chord. The more meaningful musical choices can concern the route, contiguous sequences and the approach to a phrase ending.

After introductory numeral matching, propose one or two visible musical goals with points. These are requests for the current puzzle, not an opaque judgment of whether a progression is good music. Correct joins remain non-negotiable; bonus points cannot compensate for incorrect numeral translation.

Possible goals include a specified V–I ending, IV–I ending, an ending on V, or a taught preparation–dominant–tonic sequence. Goals must be appropriate to the hand and previously introduced vocabulary. A later phrase-slot or subset-selection variant could broaden choice, but is not needed to settle the opening interaction.

## A scoring trap to avoid

Every domino already fixes one transition. If all pieces are used, summing rewards for individual transitions anywhere in the chain gives every complete ordering the same score.

For scoring to reflect player decisions, it must depend on something that changes between solutions: a longer contiguous pattern, placement at a phrase boundary, or, in a possible later variant, the subset of pieces selected.

Likewise, a generic fixed tension value for each chord is not a reliable scoring model. Musical tension depends on context, realization and expectation. Use explicit taught relationships, then let playback make their consequences audible.

## Worked musical-goal example

Key: C major. Start at C and use all seven pieces:

| Piece | Transition in this key |
| --- | --- |
| C → ii | I → ii |
| Dm → V | ii → V |
| G → I | V → I |
| C → IV | I → IV |
| F → V | IV → V |
| G → vi | V → vi |
| Am → I | vi → I |

For this example only, display two goals before play:

- **3 points:** finish with V–I.
- **2 points:** include the contiguous sequence ii–V–I, awarded once.

These are illustrative values, not settled balance constants. The bonuses deliberately stack when the requested three-chord pattern is the final arrival.

Two legal complete arrangements are:

- C–F–G–Am–C–Dm–G–C: satisfies both goals, **5 points**.
- C–Dm–G–C–F–G–Am–C: includes ii–V–I earlier but ends vi–I, **2 points**.

Both use the same transitions and finish on the same tonic. Saving G → I for the final arrival changes the result; satisfying a goal early can spend a piece needed later. Other valid arrangements must be accepted and scored by the same rules.

The second arrangement is not declared bad music. It fulfills less of this puzzle's stated request. Change the goals between puzzles rather than teaching that every phrase should maximize the same cadence pattern.

## Learning through play and progression

Begin with a small hand, one key and a reference pairing familiar chord names with their numerals. The player can reason about placement while translation is still supported. Repeated joins and playback expose root-degree and quality relationships; corrections or occasional tips can name what the player has encountered. Do not precede play with a numeral lesson.

Expanded chord-name and numeral reminders can become lighter as the corresponding relationships become accessible, while useful references remain available. Removing support is not itself a progression objective. Use both directions: interpret a numeral to find its chord, and determine which numeral a visible chord can satisfy. Do not permanently encode equivalents by matching colors or positions.

Add musical goals separately from the opening matching demand. Let the player pursue a visible goal, hear the resulting ending or preparation pattern, and encounter it across varied hands. A short contextual tip can attach a name to that observed relationship. Do not require a complete functional taxonomy merely to connect introductory dominoes.

Vary keys between puzzles without requiring simultaneous two-key coordination. Later minor and seventh-chord material needs its relevant foundations and explicit notation conventions. Avoid importing modulation, secondary dominants or a universal functional ranking into this introductory scope.

## Learning coverage and limits

- [[H2 - Roman-numeral progressions]]: translating chord names and numerals, interpreting quality, and forming ordered progressions. Chord notes and supplied playback timing are not independently demonstrated here.
- [[H4 - Tonal endings and cadences]]: proposed musical-goal forms can teach and exercise construction of endings. Merely hearing the player's completed chain is not independent recognition-by-ear evidence.
- [[H5 - Basic harmonic functions]]: proposed context-specific preparation–dominant–tonic goals after relevant H4 familiarity.
- [[H3 - Minor-key chords and progressions]]: later minor-key hands include natural-minor chords and the raised-seventh major V. In A minor, distinguish Em as v from E as V; the allowance and numeral conventions must be clear.
- [[H8 - Diatonic seventh chords]]: later major-key hands include Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7 and viiø7, including the half-diminished seventh on degree seven.

These are agreed uses of the existing whole-chord mechanic. [[Chordfall]] supplies the complementary derivation and construction forms; do not add note-building steps to domino joins or infer construction evidence from numeral matching alone.

Use relevant degree and diatonic-chord familiarity without making repeated note construction part of play. Players can bring chord knowledge from other learning or instrument experience. Numeral dominoes is distinct from [[Chordfall]] and from [[Harmony fitting]]'s choice of chords beneath a supplied melody.

## Generation, assessment and open choices

- Generate legal hands and solve them for complete chains, strategic branches and attainable goal scores. A legal-chain witness alone is not proof that the bonus challenge is meaningful.
- Include hands where different complete arrangements score differently when scoring is the focal demand. Do not claim a choice when the inventory fixes the outcome.
- Limit repeated rewards and disclose overlap rules. Points should encourage the current musical intention, not unbounded repetition of a profitable loop.
- Attribute evidence to actual player decisions and supplied support. Points, number of placements and repeated attempts are not automatically proficiency updates.
- Decide whether a legal clear passes with optional score distinction, or whether later puzzles require a disclosed target score. Keep this open until it is reconciled with lives, submission and the shared director.
- Test short, legible phone-sized hands before adding multiple phrase sections or more goal types.
- Define audition, preview and feedback so the player can hear the sequence rather than only optimize symbols.

Only the domino concept is promoted; the numerical scoring and goal-selection details above remain proposals for refinement.
